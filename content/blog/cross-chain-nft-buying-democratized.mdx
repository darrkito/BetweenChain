Pick almost any NFT marketplace and try to buy something on a chain you don't already hold funds on. You'll hit the same wall every time: connect wallet, see the NFT, see the price — and then discover you need to go somewhere else first. A centralized exchange. A bridge. Sometimes both, in sequence, before you're even allowed to click "buy."

That detour is where most people actually give up. Not because they don't want the NFT — because the number of apps, tabs, and waiting periods between "I want this" and "I own this" is high enough that momentum dies somewhere in the middle.

## The old path

Say you're holding SOL and you find a Sui collectible you want. The traditional path looks like this:

1. Find a bridge or CEX that supports Solana → Sui.
2. Send SOL there, wait for confirmations.
3. Receive SUI on the other side (minus bridge/exchange fees, and minus whatever the market moved while you waited).
4. Open a Sui-native marketplace, connect a *separate* Sui wallet.
5. Finally attempt the purchase — assuming the listing is still there and the price hasn't changed.

Every one of those steps is a place to lose money to fees, lose time to confirmations, or lose the item entirely to someone who didn't have to do any of this.

## What we built instead

A listing page on Blockchains.Click doesn't ask "do you hold the right token?" — it asks "which wallet do you want to pay from?" and quotes the purchase across chains in the same flow:

- **Buying a Sui NFT?** Pay in SUI directly, or pay in SOL, or pay in ETH — pick whichever wallet you've actually got connected and funded.
- **Buying a Solana or EVM-listed NFT?** Pay in SOL, or pay in native ETH on the NFT's own chain — same idea, same single page.

Under the hood this uses the same cross-chain quoting engine that powers [token swaps on this platform](/blog/how-cross-chain-swaps-work) — one quote, covering both the currency conversion and the chain transfer, shown to you before you commit to anything. No separate bridge step to babysit, no second wallet connection halfway through, no second app.

## Why this matters more for NFTs than for token swaps

A token is fungible — if a price moves 2% while you're bridging, you're annoyed but you still get the token. An NFT listing is not fungible: it can simply be gone, sold to someone else, by the time you've finished bridging. Every extra step between "I found it" and "I bought it" is a chance for someone with the "right" chain already funded to beat you to it. Collapsing that gap isn't a nice-to-have UX polish — it's the difference between actually being able to compete for a listing and not.

## The trust question that comes with it

Letting people pay in a different asset than the one they're receiving only works if the mechanics are verifiable, not just convenient. Every cross-chain NFT purchase here follows the same discipline [covered in our swap security writeup](/blog/swap-security-101): the destination is locked in at quote time and can't be silently altered, and the actual on-chain outcome is independently re-verified before a purchase is ever marked complete — the same standard that applies to a token swap applies to an NFT you're paying for from another chain.
