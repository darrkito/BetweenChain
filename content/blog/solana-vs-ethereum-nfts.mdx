NFT culture on Solana and Ethereum grew up separately, and it shows — the marketplaces, fee structures, buy-flow mechanics, and even the vocabulary differ more than people expect. If you're used to one chain and buying on the other for the first time, here's what actually changes.

## Marketplaces

Blockchains.Click pulls NFT listings from across every ecosystem it supports: **Magic Eden** for Solana, **OpenSea** for Ethereum and other EVM chains (including Base and ApeChain), and **Tradeport** for Sui.

You browse and buy from all three in one place, and — the actually useful part — you can pay from a different chain than the NFT itself lives on. Want a Solana NFT but you're holding ETH? You don't need to bridge and swap manually first; see [how cross-chain swaps actually work](/blog/how-cross-chain-swaps-work) for the underlying mechanism this reuses.

## How the buy flow itself differs

This is the part that surprises people more than fees do. A **same-chain** purchase — ETH in your wallet buying an Ethereum listing, or SOL buying a Solana listing — is one signature: you sign the marketplace's buy transaction directly, and there's no platform fee on that leg since no cross-chain conversion happened.

A **cross-chain** purchase is two signatures, not one: first, the paying asset is delivered to your own wallet on the NFT's chain (a real, self-custodied delivery step, not a middleman holding funds), then you separately sign the actual marketplace buy transaction once that lands. Splitting it this way exists for a real safety reason — see [Swap Security 101](/blog/swap-security-101) for why a single combined signature would have been unsafe for this specific flow.

## Fees and speed

Solana's transaction fees are a rounding error compared to Ethereum mainnet — this is why "minting culture" (rapid, cheap, often speculative minting of new collections) is so much more prevalent on Solana. Ethereum NFT activity skews toward established, higher-value collections where a larger gas fee is a smaller percentage of the transaction.

Sui, still newer to NFTs than either, has fee economics closer to Solana's — fast and cheap — with a smaller but growing collection ecosystem, and its own buy-transaction shape (Tradeport listings execute via Sui's Kiosk model, distinct from both Solana's and Ethereum's transaction formats under the hood, though you never have to think about that difference as a buyer).

## What doesn't change

Whichever chain the NFT lives on, the same protections apply: your destination/paying address is locked in at quote time and can't be altered mid-transaction, and every purchase is independently re-verified against the real on-chain result before it's marked complete — not accepted on a client-reported transaction hash alone.
