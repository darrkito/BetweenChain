NFT culture on Solana and Ethereum grew up separately, and it shows — the marketplaces, fee structures, and even the vocabulary differ more than people expect. If you're used to one chain and buying on the other for the first time, here's what actually changes.

## Marketplaces

Blockchains.Click pulls NFT listings from three vendors, each native to a different ecosystem:

- **Magic Eden** — the dominant Solana NFT marketplace.
- **OpenSea** — covers Ethereum and other EVM chains, including Base and ApeChain.
- **Tradeport** — the leading marketplace on Sui.

You browse and buy from all three in one place, and — the actually useful part — you can pay from a different chain than the NFT itself lives on. Want a Solana NFT but you're holding ETH? You don't need to bridge and swap manually first.

## Fees and speed

Solana's transaction fees are a rounding error compared to Ethereum mainnet — this is why "minting culture" (rapid, cheap, often speculative minting of new collections) is so much more prevalent on Solana. Ethereum NFT activity skews toward established, higher-value collections where a larger gas fee is a smaller percentage of the transaction.

Sui, still newer to NFTs than either, has fee economics closer to Solana's — fast and cheap — with a smaller but growing collection ecosystem via Tradeport.

## What doesn't change

Whichever chain the NFT lives on, the same protections apply: your destination address is locked in at quote time and can't be altered mid-transaction, and every purchase is independently re-verified against the real on-chain result before it's marked complete.
