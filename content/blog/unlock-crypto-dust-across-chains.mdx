Open a wallet you've used for more than a few months and you'll probably find the same thing: a long tail of tiny balances you don't remember acquiring and definitely don't remember why you're still holding. A few dollars of a meme coin that never went anywhere. Fractions of a token from an airdrop you forgot you qualified for. The leftover crumb from a swap that didn't round evenly.

Individually, none of it looks worth the effort. Added up across every chain you've ever touched, it often is.

## Why dust exists in the first place

It's not really your fault — the tools that create dust are the same ones that make crypto useful in the first place:

- **Swaps leave remainders.** Trade a token for another and you rarely land on an exact, clean number. The leftover sits in your wallet as its own tiny balance.
- **Airdrops are unpredictable.** Projects distribute tokens to wallets that meet some criteria, whether or not you ever plan to use that token. Most of these never accrue meaningful value, but they still show up.
- **Rug tokens go to zero, but the account doesn't disappear.** A token can lose all its value and still leave a real, on-chain footprint behind.
- **Bridges have minimums.** Most cross-chain routes reject anything below a certain dollar threshold — the exact amounts most dust falls under — so it just sits there, un-movable through normal means.

None of this is a bug. It's just a side effect of how often you've actually used your wallet.

## The part that's easy to miss: rent

If you've ever held tokens on Solana, this part is worth knowing specifically. Every token you hold there lives in its own dedicated account, and opening that account requires a small SOL deposit — a few thousandths of a SOL, intentionally, to keep the network's storage costs covered. That deposit isn't a fee. It's refundable, in full, the moment the account is closed.

Most people never close these accounts, because most wallets never surface the option. Every one of them left open is real SOL sitting unclaimed.

## What actually helps

Two different problems, two different fixes — and both are worth doing at once:

1. **Consolidate small balances into one token you actually want to hold.** Instead of tracking a dozen sub-$5 positions across three chains, sweep them into a single asset — native SOL, ETH, or a stablecoin — in one guided pass.
2. **Reclaim rent from accounts that are already empty.** If a token balance has already hit zero, there's no swap to make — just an account to close and SOL to get back.

Both together tend to turn "a wallet full of stuff I don't remember" into a number that's actually worth having.

## A realistic expectation

Sweeping dust isn't free — each token still needs its own small transaction to move, the same way any swap does. For genuinely tiny amounts (think fractions of a cent) the cost of moving it can exceed what it's worth, and that's fine to just leave alone. The value is in the balances that add up to real money once you actually look at them together, which is more often the case than most people expect.

Check what's actually sitting in your wallet before assuming it's not worth the five minutes.
